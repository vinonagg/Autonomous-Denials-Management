# LinkedIn announcement

Draft copy for announcing the concept and prototype.

---

Denials have quietly become a board-level number.

Around 11% of claims are denied on first pass.
Most are preventable. Roughly half are never reworked.

For a mid-sized health system, that's tens of millions in margin — gone, every year. And it gets worse as payers automate their side of the process.

So I built something.

Over the past few weeks I designed — and prototyped — an Agentic AI approach to denials. I'm calling it Autonomous Denials Management.

The concept is simple: a supervised fleet of AI agents that works a denied claim end to end.

→ Classify the remittance and route it
→ Reconstruct the true root cause from the record
→ Ground the case in the payer's current medical policy
→ Assemble the minimum-necessary clinical evidence
→ Choose the play — appeal, corrected claim, peer-to-peer
→ Draft the submission, every citation verified

A human owns every irreversible step.
Autonomy is earned one denial category at a time, and only after measured accuracy.
Medical-necessity judgment never leaves a clinician.

What it changes, in my model:

- Denials worked: ~60% → 95%+. Nothing is ever "too small to pursue."
- Appeal cycle time: 30+ days → under 7.
- First-pass overturn: up 10–15 points — every appeal policy-cited and evidence-backed.
- A prevention loop that feeds root causes back to scheduling, authorization and coding, so the initial denial rate actually falls.

The point isn't replacing the denials team.
It's giving them capacity to work the backlog no one has time for — and moving experienced specialists into prevention and exception handling.
